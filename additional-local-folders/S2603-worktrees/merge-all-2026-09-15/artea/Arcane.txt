ARCANE-INSPIRED PIXEL ARCHITECTURE BLUEPRINT FOR ARTEA
Version: 1.0
Date: 2026-08-25
Scope: reusable environment, architecture, prop, lighting, VFX, and animation rules

PURPOSE

This document translates the visual-production principles of the animated series
Arcane into an original pixel-art architecture system for Artea. It is not a
pixel-perfect copy of any Arcane frame, building, character, logo, graffiti, or
prop. Arcane is painterly hybrid 2D/3D animation, not pixel art. Every pixel-art
rule below is an original game-design inference built from documented production
principles and the existing Artea architecture blueprints.

Use this blueprint for:
- stations, platforms, bridges, stairs, plazas, and guard systems;
- castles, civic buildings, laboratories, workshops, and houses;
- floating islands, undercrofts, foundations, machines, and vehicles;
- props, signs, doors, windows, furniture, and interaction markers;
- background districts, skyline landmarks, and world transitions;
- Web-safe Godot materials, animation, lighting, and VFX.

Do not use it to reproduce protected Arcane characters, symbols, exact buildings,
scene compositions, story elements, or graffiti. Build an original Artea visual
identity from the reusable principles.


1. RESEARCH FINDINGS: WHAT ARCANE ACTUALLY DOES

1.1 World design communicates story

- Piltover and Zaun are deliberately different visual narratives.
- Piltover's established shape language is geometry, symmetry, and mechanical
  beauty. It is modern and clean, but also grounded, weathered, and visibly old
  enough to feel inhabited.
- Piltover uses strong verticals and ordered geometry associated with Art Deco.
- Zaun acts as a dark mirror: Victorian and industrial components, handcrafted
  upcycled construction, asymmetric flourish, Gothic influence, and organic
  beauty rather than civic purity.
- Landmarks orient the viewer and carry story meaning. Repeated generic clutter
  is less valuable than one readable clock tower, bridge, gate, reactor, or sign.
- A visual library of compatible buildings, stores, props, and set components
  lets individual streets feel unique while sharing one shape language.

1.2 Surface and image style

- Fortiche combines 3D staging with painted backgrounds, matte painting, and 2D
  effects. Graphic scratches, textures, and imperfections help the image feel
  drawn and raw rather than uniformly computer-clean.
- Detail is simplified and composed. Appeal comes from graphic shape, texture,
  and painting rather than from covering every surface with small objects.
- A first sketch can carry useful energy; iteration should preserve that clear
  first idea rather than polish it into visual noise.
- Composition, camera angle, framing, pose, and lighting intention are planned
  early. An object is designed for how it reads in the final view.

1.3 Color and light

- Color and lighting are narrative systems, selected from mood boards and kept
  coherent across sequences.
- Dark passages alternate with focused high-contrast moments. Bright energy is
  meaningful because it is not equally bright everywhere.
- Piltover's useful game translation is pale mineral, blue-green metal, deep
  blue shadow, controlled brass, and blue Hextech-like energy.
- Zaun's useful game translation is soot, old iron, oxidized green, toxic lime,
  bruised violet, stained amber, and irregular local light.
- Materials must remain distinguishable by value before hue: light stone, middle
  metal, dark recess, bright energy, and small warm accent.

1.4 Animation and effects

- Arcane animation prioritizes readable composition and restrained, believable
  movement instead of constant motion or exaggerated overacting.
- 2D effects, scratches, painted accents, and graphic shapes sit over 3D motion.
- Moving parts should reveal purpose: gears transfer power, pistons reciprocate,
  conduits pulse toward a destination, and lights signal state.
- Environment motion is selective. A still structure with one moving mechanism
  reads more strongly than an entire building moving without hierarchy.


2. ARTEA PIXEL-ART TRANSLATION

2.1 Pixel grid

- Author environment textures on a 16 px base module.
- Use 8 px for small props, 16 px for standard architectural tiles, 32 px for
  large facade modules, and 64 px for hero floor or wall motifs.
- Snap primary edges to a 2 px sub-grid. Reserve isolated 1 px marks for wear,
  sparks, highlights, and silhouette correction.
- Never scatter single pixels uniformly. Cluster pixels into intentional chips,
  brush marks, rivets, or light blooms.
- Use nearest-neighbour filtering. Do not blur pixel textures.

2.2 Value hierarchy

Every asset must read in grayscale using five roles:

1. Deep recess: 10-18% lightness.
2. Structural shadow: 20-32%.
3. Main material: 38-58%.
4. Edge plane: 62-78%.
5. Energy/specular focal point: 82-100%.

Use no more than three large value masses in one ordinary asset. The fourth and
fifth roles are accents, not equal-area fills.

2.3 Shape hierarchy

- Start with one silhouette statement.
- Divide it into three large architectural masses.
- Add one repeated structural rhythm.
- Add one focal mechanism, entrance, window group, or emblem.
- Add weathering last and never use it to repair weak geometry.

Piltover module language:
- bilateral symmetry or carefully balanced near-symmetry;
- stepped Art Deco crowns and chevrons;
- tall piers, recessed panels, fan motifs, diamonds, and sunbursts;
- circles used as mechanisms inside rectilinear frames;
- clean spans with visible bearings and load paths;
- brass only at joints, frames, thresholds, controls, and focal inlays.

Zaun module language:
- controlled asymmetry around a stable load-bearing core;
- reused plates, pipes, braces, vents, and suspended walkways;
- Gothic points and Art Nouveau curves made from practical industrial parts;
- local repairs and material changes that tell construction history;
- bright chemical light contained in pipes, tanks, windows, or work zones.

2.4 Anti-clutter law

For each 64 x 64 architectural tile:
- 70% quiet primary material;
- 20% structural paneling or shadow;
- at most 10% warm metal or high-energy accent.

Do not stack multiple full-size floor meshes to indicate gameplay zones. Use one
walkable surface, clear openings, signs, edge frames, and an authored texture.
Do not place decoration in a player route, doorway projection, stair head, train
clearance envelope, or moving-object sweep.
Do not add a brace, ring, pipe, crystal, gear, or light without a named purpose.
When two decorative systems occupy the same plane, keep the more informative one.

TRANSITION / SHARED ARCANE TECHNOLOGY
- Keep the structure in navy, iron, and pale mineral.
- Let district identity enter through secondary metal, window light, signage,
  surface wear, and one energy color.
- Never use rainbow distribution across architecture. Rainbow belongs to a hero
  magical object or route and should be framed by a stable neutral structure.


4. PIXEL MATERIAL RECIPES

Pale mineral:
- 4 colors: shadow, body, worn edge, highlight.
- Use broad 2-4 px planar brush clusters.
- Break long bright edges every 12-20 px to avoid a plastic white line.

Navy painted metal:
- 4 colors plus optional rust/brass chip.
- Use 1 px highlight only on top-facing or focal edges.
- Put scratches in clusters of 2-5 pixels and align some with use direction.

Oxidized teal metal:
- Use dark blue shadow under the teal; do not shade toward gray.
- Patina collects under joints and around water paths.
- Keep the center of panels quieter than their frames.

Brass/gold:
- Use dark brown, antique brass, warm gold, and one pale highlight.
- Limit continuous gold lines. Break or step them at structural bays.
- Gold must explain hierarchy: threshold, bearing, control, crown, or focal icon.

Energy cyan:
- Use deep teal casing, cyan core, and a tiny near-white center.
- Emission belongs inside an engineered channel, not across an entire floor.
- Pulse along a path toward a mechanism; do not flash every segment together.

Glass:
- Use dark transparent body with selective cyan edge pixels.
- Reflections should be sparse diagonal clusters, not full white stripes.
- Glass frames carry collision; glass panes remain visual.


5. ARCHITECTURE KIT

5.1 Platform and station

- One continuous floor surface per platform side.
- One dark structural deck beneath it.
- Recessed teal facade panels on a consistent bay spacing.
- Broken/stepped brass threshold lines rather than one white underside stripe.
- Three doorway-aligned gate openings: one driver and two passenger.
- Floor motif: large mirrored Deco frame, central cyan circuit, brass corner
  keystones, quiet navy field, and sparse painterly wear.
- Roof loads travel through bearing beam -> column -> plinth -> drop pier ->
  island/hull. No loose horizontal blocks beside foundations.

5.2 Bridge and stair

- Deck, stair, and platform share the same material family and pixel density.
- Keep a clear central travel lane.
- Put Art Deco rhythm in the edge frame and landing, not on every stair tread.
- A bridge must visibly land on a bearing or foundation; never float near it.
- Remove energy strips that visually pass through the foundation or terminate in
  empty space.

5.3 Civic building

- Three-part facade: grounded base, ordered middle bays, stepped crown.
- Use a centered entrance and vertical window grouping.
- One landmark element per building: clock, dome, tower, bridge, observatory, or
  mechanical rose window.
- Add subtle wear at ground contact, drains, hinges, and traffic zones.

5.4 Workshop / laboratory

- Expose only the machine parts needed to explain power flow.
- Group pipes into manifolds rather than scattering individual tubes.
- Give each animated mechanism an input, transformation, and output.
- Keep a dark backing plane behind bright gears or crystals for silhouette.

5.5 Floating island / undercroft

- Read as 3-5 large horizontal or radial masses, not many colored triangles.
- Top crown supports traversal; middle band carries civic structure; deep taper
  carries weight toward the core.
- Ribs repeat at a deterministic interval and use one material family.
- Use one narrow brass crown band and one cyan energy belt at different heights.

5.6 Natural and Tree of Life integration

- Preserve the existing rule that trunk, roots, and structural ridges form one
  continuous load-bearing mass.
- Mechanical collars attach only where wood mass can plausibly support them.
- Cyan stays in biological energy paths; antique gold stays on mechanisms.
- Keep heart, doorway, and projection corridors free of foliage and ornament.


6. PIXEL ANIMATION BLUEPRINT

Static architecture:
- 0 fps. Let lighting and one focal mechanism carry life.

Ambient mechanism:
- Author at 12 fps or animate on every second rendered frame.
- Use 4, 6, 8, or 12-frame loops.
- Gear rotation must match connected gear ratios and direction.
- Piston and rod endpoints remain attached throughout the loop.

Energy:
- Use a 6-8 frame traveling pulse, not whole-object blinking.
- Offset repeated conduits so the pulse reads as flow.
- Idle energy range: 15-35% brightness variation.
- Alert/action range: one quick 70-100% peak followed by recovery.

Smoke/steam:
- 6-10 frame shape loop with pixel-cluster expansion.
- Move the mass first, then add only a few detached pixels at dissipation.
- Use dark-to-light value change before transparency fade.

Impact and magic FX:
- One anticipation frame, one high-contrast contact frame, 2-4 decay frames.
- Use graphic 2D overlays for sparks, scratches, speed lines, and impact shapes.
- Keep FX outside text, face, doorway, and interaction-sign readability zones.

Camera and presentation:
- Compose landmark, route, and focal light before adding detail.
- Use strong foreground/midground/background value separation.
- Reserve camera shake for impacts; ordinary mechanisms should not shake the view.


7. GODOT IMPLEMENTATION CONTRACT

- Use one collision owner per continuous walkable route where practical.
- Visual pixel patterns use a single nearest-filtered texture/material, not
  stacked coplanar meshes.
- Visual ornament has no collision unless it is a real barrier or support.
- Use named semantic nodes: Structure, WalkableSurface, Frame, EnergyPath,
  Mechanism, Bearing, Support, Collision.
- Keep Web materials simple: StandardMaterial3D or Web-safe spatial shaders.
- Avoid runtime booleans, displacement, excessive transparent layers, and large
  particle counts.
- Prefer instanced modules and deterministic counts.
- Separate static architecture from animated pivots.
- Every moving part has a bounded loop and a documented purpose.
- Every platform and bridge change must re-run traversal, station, train, gallery,
  minimap, Web export, and production browser checks required by AGENTS.md.


8. RELATION TO EXISTING ARTEA BLUEPRINTS

This blueprint preserves the strongest existing contracts:

- Art Castle Magitech Research: visible load paths; integrated light technology;
  total-environment detail instead of loose decoration; clear walkable routes.
- Castle Entrance Reengineering: consistent datums, unobstructed axis, restrained
  element counts, and correct causeway/terrace handoff.
- Tree of Life blueprints: named modules and sockets; organic/mechanical clear
  zones; limited gold/cyan roles; static architecture with bounded focal motion.
- Crown of Life implementation: low-poly/Web-safe presentation, gameplay-first
  collision, accessible routes, and deterministic tests.
- Mind Spark design: technology is readable through purpose and progression, not
  through random density.

When a future blueprint conflicts with this file, gameplay clearance, structural
continuity, authored story meaning, accessibility, and Web performance take
priority over style.


9. REVIEW CHECKLIST

Silhouette
[ ] One clear large idea is readable at thumbnail scale.
[ ] Architecture has 1-3 dominant masses, not a pile of equal objects.
[ ] Landmark and entrance are visible from the intended player route.

Structure
[ ] Roof, bridge, platform, and machine loads have visible support paths.
[ ] No beam, pipe, brace, or line ends in empty space.
[ ] No decorative object overlaps another structural object accidentally.

Pixel art
[ ] All primary edges use the selected grid and nearest filtering.
[ ] Pixel clusters are intentional; there is no uniform single-pixel noise.
[ ] Materials read by value before hue.
[ ] Wear is localized and supports history.

Color
[ ] Gold/brass occupies at most 10% of ordinary architecture.
[ ] Cyan emission occupies at most 4% unless it is the focal magical object.
[ ] Large surfaces use navy, teal, mineral, wood, stone, or iron fields.
[ ] No competing rainbow panels appear on ordinary civic foundations.

Gameplay
[ ] Player route and all doorway projections remain clear.
[ ] Train, vehicle, gear, and animated sweep volumes remain clear.
[ ] Collision is simple, continuous, and independent from visual ornament.

Animation
[ ] Every moving part communicates a function.
[ ] Motion uses readable, bounded loops and does not animate everything.
[ ] FX preserve route, sign, UI, and character readability.

Performance
[ ] Materials and shaders work in the Godot Web renderer.
[ ] Transparent layers and particles are bounded.
[ ] Repeated modules are instanced or generated deterministically.
[ ] Required native and production Web tests pass.
