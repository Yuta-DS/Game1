KILN — World Concept

World concept · placement puzzler

KILN.

A drowned ceramics works where the world is unfinished clay. You carry a glaze plate. Press it to a view and that view fires into a tile. Set the tile down and what it holds becomes real, load-bearing, walkable.

Puzzles are made of missing things — a stair that stops, a gap over the slip-pools, a door with no room behind it. You fix them by firing a piece of somewhere else and setting it into the hole.

Four regions · a firing sequence

The verb

  1. Frame — hold up the plate; the view inside its border dims to greenware grey.
  2. Fire — the plate flashes kiln-amber and the framed view sets as a solid tile.
  3. Set — carry the tile, align it to a gap; edges that match snap and fuse.
  4. Break — strike a fired tile to return it to the plate, at the cost of one firing.

Original concept. Flat-shaded geometry, hard shadows, a warm bone void instead of a sky. The palette is earthen throughout — terracotta, oxide copper, iron and kiln amber — so the fired tiles read as ceramic against it rather than as photographs.
